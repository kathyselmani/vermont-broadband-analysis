Key findings

The infrastructure is mostly there. Choice and adoption lag.

Nine in ten Vermont homes can order 100/20 Mbps service. But most have one wired provider or none, rural areas still have real gaps, and lower-income areas subscribe less even where service exists.

Method & data use

How to read this, and what it does not show

Definitions

  • Served: a reliable, low-latency offer of at least 100 Mbps down and 20 up.
  • Underserved: best reliable offer between 25/3 and 100/20. Unserved: nothing reliable at 25/3.
  • Reliable (federal BEAD rule): fiber, cable, DSL and licensed fixed wireless. Satellite and unlicensed wireless don't count.
  • Universe: 282,377 locations with at least one residential offer.
  • Need index: equal-weight average of four indicators, each scaled 0 (best county) to 1 (worst).

Privacy

  • No addresses, coordinates, household data or FCC location IDs appear on this page or in the published files.
  • The smallest unit shown is an H3 hexagon of about 0.7 km². Hexagons with fewer than 5 locations show no count, and their colors come from the surrounding area (about 5 km²).
  • Provider lists are shown for zones of about 5 km². Zones with fewer than 5 locations show the wider area (about 36 km²) instead.
  • Census figures are published estimates for whole tracts, counties and towns.
  • This page uses no cookies, analytics or tracking.
